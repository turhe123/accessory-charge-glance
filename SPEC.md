# AccessoryChargeGlance — specification

**Tagline:** See the battery level of your Magic Mouse, keyboard, trackpad and other Bluetooth gear in the menu bar, with low alerts.

**Category:** Battery & Power

**Inspired by (paid):** AirBuddy ($9.99 one-time)

**Frameworks:** SwiftUI, AppKit, IOKit, IOBluetooth, UserNotifications, ServiceManagement

**Donation URL (Support button):** https://ko-fi.com/leodavidai

## Spec

What the paid app does and the demand. AirBuddy is a long-running Mac utility that shows the battery level of connected accessories such as the Magic Mouse, Magic Keyboard, Magic Trackpad, AirPods and other Bluetooth peripherals from the menu bar, and warns you when one runs low. Macworld reviewed it at a price of 9.99 dollars one time, with a license covering several Macs, and it is also offered through Setapp. It keeps appearing in recommendation threads and lists of must-have menu bar tools because macOS only shows accessory batteries buried in the Bluetooth menu or a Notification Center widget. Several smaller paid and free clones exist, which confirms steady demand. Our clean-room version focuses only on the battery readout and alerts, not on device handoff or pairing.

Feature set for our version. A menu bar item shows a small icon plus the lowest battery percentage among connected accessories, or optionally a compact row of icons. Clicking it opens a popover listing each connected accessory with its name, a device type symbol, a battery bar, the percentage, and a charging indicator when the device reports one. Devices are sorted with the lowest battery first. A low battery threshold, default 20 percent, triggers a local notification once per drop below the threshold, and a second optional notification at a critical level of 10 percent. A notification also fires when a device reaches full charge if the user enables that option. The app remembers the last known level of each device so that a disconnected accessory appears in a dimmed Recently seen section with a timestamp. Roughly 350 to 500 lines of Swift in total.

UI spec. This is a MenuBarExtra app with the window style so the popover can hold a list. The header shows the title and a refresh button. Each row has the device name, the percentage and a colored bar that is green above the threshold, amber near it and red below it. The footer has a Settings button and a Quit button. The empty state reads No battery-reporting accessories found, with a hint to connect a Bluetooth device. The Settings window, opened with Command comma, has these fields: low threshold slider from 5 to 50 percent, critical threshold slider, toggle for full charge alerts, toggle to show percentage text in the menu bar, toggle to show icons for every device versus only the lowest, refresh interval picker with 1, 2 and 5 minutes, per-device mute list with a switch for each known device, and a launch at login toggle.

Technical approach. Battery data for Apple and many third party Bluetooth HID devices is exposed through the IOKit registry. The app iterates the IORegistry for services of class AppleDeviceManagementHIDEventService and IOBluetoothDevice style entries, and reads properties such as Product, BatteryPercent, DeviceAddress and BatteryStatusFlags using IORegistryEntryCreateCFProperties. For paired devices not exposed that way, the app consults IOBluetooth paired devices to get names and connection state, and shows them without a percentage rather than guessing. Polling runs on a timer using the selected interval and also when IOBluetooth connect and disconnect notifications arrive, so new devices appear promptly. State is held in an observable model that maps each device address to its latest reading, and the last known readings are persisted as a small JSON file in Application Support. Settings use AppStorage. Notifications use UserNotifications with authorization requested on first launch of the alert feature, and each alert is deduplicated by device address and threshold crossing, resetting when the level rises above the threshold by at least 5 points or the device charges. Launch at login uses SMAppService mainApp register and unregister. No Accessibility, Screen Recording or Microphone permission is needed. Bluetooth permission may be prompted by the system the first time IOBluetooth is used, and the app explains why in its usage description string.

Edge cases and error handling. Some devices report no battery value they are listed as Battery not reported. AirPods are not covered by the registry in the same way, so the app states plainly in its About text that only devices macOS exposes through IOKit are shown. A battery value of zero while connected is treated as unknown for the first few seconds after connection to avoid false low alerts. If the Bluetooth permission is denied, the popover shows an explanation with a button that opens the Privacy settings pane. Devices with duplicate names are disambiguated by the last digits of the address. On Macs without Bluetooth the empty state explains this. Notification permission denial is detected and a banner in Settings explains how to enable it. Stale readings older than 30 minutes for connected devices are marked with a small clock symbol.
